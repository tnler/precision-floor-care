02 — The difference

A mop moves dirt.
We take it with us.

This is the part most people never see, and it's the entire reason a floor looks different after we leave than it does after a mop-and-bucket night.

Everything is sucked up.

Solution goes down, the machine agitates, and the same pass vacuums all of it back off the floor. Whatever came off leaves the building in the recovery tank — not sitting in a low spot, not drying into a film, not waiting in the grout line for the next damp day.

No pushing dirty water.

A mop bucket gets dirtier with every room, and after the second one you're spreading the first room across the rest of the floor. Nothing here gets rinsed in water that's already been used — the dirty side never touches the floor again.

03 — Process

Three steps. Every job.

Same sequence whether it's a lobby or a warehouse. The order matters — soil has to be loosened before it can be lifted, and lifted before it can be removed.

Step 01

Wash

Solution goes down across the whole area and gets time to work. Grease, traffic film, and ground-in soil soften and release instead of being scoured while dry.

Step 02

Strip

Machine agitation breaks the bond between the floor and everything sitting in it — including the build-up down in the grout lines and along the edges where mops never reach.

Step 03

Clean

All of it gets vacuumed back up in the same pass. The floor is left clean and dry enough to walk on, and every bit of the dirty water goes out the door with us.
